Every payment has someone on the other end, and omsync calls that the other side. The contact that stands for you is the owner. The first name you add on the Contacts page creates the owner, so your own names are the first thing to enter.

Your page holds your names, saved the way banks print them, and any accounts you have outside omsync. It has no balance, and it cannot be merged or deleted.

{{ figure("contacts-list", "The Contacts page: you first, then each contact with its kind, balance, and open items.", "The Contacts list has you first, then each contact and what is owed between you.") }} {% endcall %} {% call section("contacts", "Contacts and their names") %}

A contact is a person or business you know by name. In the demo, Sam Park and Priya Nair are contacts. Each contact has:

  • Names, in parts: a given name, an optional middle name, and an optional family name. The forms that banks print are derived from them. Sam Park is recognized as SAM PARK, PARK SAM, S PARK, PARK S, and SAM P.
  • Patterns, for wordings that no name covers.
  • Accounts outside the ledger, with a label and optionally the last four digits.

Every change to a contact is a batch, so it can be previewed and undone like any other change. Deleting a contact removes its names, patterns, and accounts in the same step, and undo restores them.

After every import and sync, omsync recognizes the other side of each transaction you have not set yourself. It tries these in order and stops at the first one that finds someone:

{% call steps() %} {% call step("A contact's account, by its digits") %}

A description that names the last four digits of an account a contact holds, or a pattern tied to that account.

{% endcall %} {% call step("A payment that names a contact") %}

A Zelle payment, or another payment wording, whose name is a saved contact's name, such as Zelle payment to PRIYA NAIR.

{% endcall %} {% call step("A contact's pattern") %}

A pattern on the side the sign names: money out or money in.

{% endcall %} {% call step("A pass-through") %}

A transfer to one of your accounts of exactly the amount that one contact paid you within seven days before or after it. The money was theirs, so they are the other side.

{% endcall %} {% endcall %}

If a step finds more than one contact, omsync names nobody, and no later step guesses. Recognition never sets a split, and it never replaces a choice you made. You can set the other side yourself on a transaction's detail page, or for several rows at once on Reconcile.

{% endcall %} {% call section("balances", "Balances: what a contact owes you") %}

Each contact other than you has a balance: what is owed between you and that contact. Only money in a category of type Owed counts, such as Loans. Spending and income are settled when they are paid, so they never count toward a balance.

In the demo you lent Priya Nair $300, and she has paid back $150. Both are Loans, so the balance is $150 and the Contacts page shows that Priya owes you it. The repayment clears part of the loan, and the balance view lists what is still open.

{% call callout("tip", "Money you hold for someone") %}

A reimbursement with nothing owed against it stays open. It is money you hold for the contact, until the contact's share of the original purchase is recorded as a split that names them.

{% endcall %} {{ try_it("/settings/contacts", "Open Contacts", "The list shows each balance and its direction: the contact owes you, you owe them, or it is settled.") }} {% endcall %} {% call section("unmatched", "When nobody is found") %}

A payment from a person you have no contact for is still explained, by the name as written. In the demo, Taylor Kim sent $45 by Zelle, and no contact covers that name. Its other side reads Other side: TAYLOR KIM on the Reconcile page, under Explained. Nothing is stored for that name, so the payment has no contact yet.

Saving the person as a contact changes that at once: their payments are stored with that contact as the other side. You can also set the other side for one payment, or for several at a time, with What is on the other side of the selected rows? on Reconcile. A new contact made there takes its name as written: the first word is the given name, and the last word is the family name.

{% call callout("careful", "Money that is your own") %}

A payment that reads as your own money, by your name or by a tracked account, is never explained by a name. If one is left unlinked, it is listed under Not matched, and that is an error to fix. Set its other side to one of your own accounts outside the ledger, or link it.
