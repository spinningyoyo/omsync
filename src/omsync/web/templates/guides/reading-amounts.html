Every amount is a signed number of cents, and its sign says which way the money moved. A negative amount is money out. A positive amount is money in.

A credit card follows the same rule. A purchase on a card is money out of the card, so it is negative. A payment that clears the card is money in to the card, so it is positive. The same payment is money out of the checking account that sent it.

The balance omsync shows is the bank's ledger balance as reported on a date. It is not the available balance, which some banks also report, and omsync does not put one in its place. An account page shows the balance together with its date.

A checking account's balance is money you have. A credit card's balance is what you owe, and it keeps the sign the statement reports. In the demo, a card you owe shows a negative balance, and it goes back toward zero when the autopay clears it.

The Accounts panel groups accounts by type. Each group has a total, which sums the balances it knows. If you choose to group by institution instead, the panel shows no totals, because netting a card's debt against a checking balance would mean nothing.

Reports count money by its category. The category's type decides whether it is income, an expense, or left out. This is the same for every view that shows a total, so the dashboard, budgets, and reports agree.

Two details matter when you read a total. First, a transfer may be only partly explained. If a $100 outflow has $40 explained by a transfer link, reports count the remaining $60. Second, joint accounts count at half by default, which is the joint share setting. It applies once per transaction, and it changes reports, not the balance the bank reported.

The Privacy mode button in the top bar blurs the amounts and charts on the screen. Use it when someone is looking over your shoulder. It only changes what is drawn: the amounts are still on the page, and exports are still readable. It is not a lock, so it does not replace signing out.

{% call callout("tip", "Quick check") %}

If an amount looks wrong, look at its sign first. A transfer between your own accounts appears twice, as money out of one account and money in to the other. Its category is Transfer, which is Excluded, so reports leave it out and the money is counted once.
