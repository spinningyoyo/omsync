omsync is a personal finance tracker that runs on this Mac, and your data stays here. Signing in uses your Bitwarden account, and bank sync, if you set it up, visits your bank's website. You bring in the statement files your banks give you. omsync keeps a ledger, one record for each movement of money in your accounts, and builds on it: where money went, how transfers between your own accounts line up, and what you plan to spend.

It follows one rule: trust before convenience. An import keeps the evidence from each statement. Automation never replaces a choice you made. When omsync is unsure, it asks you instead of guessing. The server listens on 127.0.0.1, so only this Mac can reach it.

Most of the time you do two things: bring in statements, then categorize what they contain. Reconciling related money is a separate, less frequent review, and you do not have to finish it before the ledger is useful.

Every page has the same frame. On a wide screen it is three parts:

  • The rail, down the left edge. It holds Home, Reports, Transactions, Recurring, Reconcile, and Plan. On a phone the same items sit in a bar along the bottom.
  • The Accounts panel, beside the page. It lists every open account with its latest balance, grouped by type or by institution. The button at the top left of the page opens and closes it.
  • The page itself, with the top bar above it. The top bar has a Guides link back to these walkthroughs, a Privacy mode button, which blurs amounts, and, on a phone, the user menu.

The user menu sits at the foot of the rail, or in the top bar on a phone. It holds Guides, Settings, Bank sync, a Scene item that slides out the scene choices, which also tint the page, the auto, day, and night theme choice, and Sign out. Configuration you set up once, such as your categories and keywords, lives under Settings rather than on the rail.

The demo follows Kunchorn Omsin, a made-up person, through a year of money. Everything in it is invented: the names, the numbers, and the banks' descriptions. Kunchorn has:

  • a Bank of America checking account, Everyday Checking, and two savings accounts, Emergency Fund and Travel Savings;
  • a Chase card, Sapphire Card, and a Citi card, Double Cash, both paid from checking each month;
  • a paycheck every other Friday from Northwind Labs, and a roommate, Sam Park, who pays half the rent by Zelle;
  • a friend, Priya Nair, who borrowed $300 and has paid back $150;
  • a summer trip to Portland, paid on the Double Cash card.

The other guides point at these examples, so you can see each idea on real-looking data before you use your own.

Nothing here leaves this Mac. Everyone using this server sees the same demo, and it starts over when the server stops. The Demo pill in the top bar is a reminder of that. Use Reset demo in the user menu to put Kunchorn's year back as it was after you have changed things.
