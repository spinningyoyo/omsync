{% extends "layouts/guide.html" %} {% from "guides/parts.html" import section, figure, try_it, compare_table with context %} {% set sections = [ ("what", "What omsync does"), ("amounts", "Reading amounts"), ("demo", "The demo"), ] %} {% block guide_body %} {% call section("what", "What omsync does") %}
  • It keeps a ledger from your bank statements: one record for each transaction.
  • It sorts transactions into categories, links transfers between your accounts, and plans budgets and goals.
  • It never changes a category that you set. When it is not sure, it asks you.
  • You can undo each change from the message that follows it. Undo never overwrites a later edit.
  • Undo does not cover display settings, statement files, or anything at your bank.
  • Your data stays on this Mac. Only this Mac can open omsync, and nothing goes to its authors.

  • Negative is money out. Positive is money in.
  • A card balance that you owe is negative.
  • An account's balance is the balance on the bank's last statement, with its date.
  • A transfer between your accounts shows in both accounts. Reports leave it out.
  • Joint accounts count at half in reports. Balances stay as the bank reports them.
  • Privacy mode in the top bar blurs amounts. It does not lock omsync.
